[ SEC.07 / OBJECTIONS ]

Asked and answered.

"I'm locked into a contract."

Maybe, maybe not. Send the agreement with the statement and I'll read the term and the early-termination clause for you. If leaving costs more than switching saves, I'll say so and we wait for the term to end. If it doesn't, we talk about who eats the fee.

"My bank does it."

Banks resell processing; they don't run it, and the rate is rarely the one you'd get if anyone were competing for the account. Send the statement. If the bank is genuinely good, that's a two-line email from me saying keep it.

"Rates always creep up."

They do when nobody is reading the statement. Mine come with a check: your first statement on the new account gets compared to your last one on the old, line by line, and you get that comparison in writing. After that, tell me when a number moves and I'll find out why.

"What's your cut?"

I'm an agent for a processor. I earn a share of the processing on accounts I place, which is why the review costs you nothing and why I'd rather tell you to keep what you have than move you onto something that doesn't beat it.

"Will my customers notice anything?"

A different terminal or a payment link, that's all. Card acceptance works the same way from their side. If you invoice, they get a link they can pay from their phone; if you're on site, they tap.

"Do I need new equipment?"

Usually one device or none: a tap-to-pay terminal for the truck, or nothing at all if you invoice and take deposits by link. What it costs, if anything, is on the comparison before you decide.

"Is this safe?"

The processor handles card data, not me and not your website; that's how it should be, and it's why I won't build a form that stores a card number. Invoices and deposit links are hosted by the processor, and you never touch the card.

"What happens after I say yes?"

An application, a signature, and the account is usually live within days. Equipment ships or a payment link goes on your estimates. Then the first-statement check, in writing, so we both know it worked.
